# Design notes

> **The game is now the d6 chapter dice system.** Its current rules, balance numbers, open items and a dated record of every pass live in [`game/README.md`](../game/README.md). The balance targets and most decisions below are for the **legacy d20 build**. The setting, the persistence row, comebacks at full strength, the restraint pillar and the only-flip-the-top-card rule carry over.

## What it is

An original co-op card game by Binary Defense, built to be handed out at conferences and played at the booth. It deliberately follows the structure of *Escape the Dark Castle* (one card at a time, boss underneath, simultaneous rolls assigned to enemies, doubles that block, a kit card after each fight). Every rule, card, and word of text is original. **Counsel review is required before anything ships**, because the structural resemblance is close.

**Taken from Escape the Dark Castle:** one card at a time with the boss underneath, no turn order, effects that hit whoever is on point, simultaneous rolls assigned to the enemy, doubles that hit twice and block, every survivor dealing damage, one player resting per round, a kit card after every fight, losing if anyone falls.

**Ours:** the d20 instead of symbol dice; one shuffled situation pool instead of chapters; the always-on persistence row that rolls with every flip; comebacks that strike on arrival (the two biggest per roll); dual-mode AI agents in a shared crew tray; crew-size scaling through dice and alarm hits instead of HP tables; eight alarm keywords; the SOC setting and art.

## Balance targets (legacy d20)

Tuned with `sim/` against a careful bot, never yet against enough humans.

| Mode | Target win rate | Shipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full Shift, Recruit | 60–70% | 62 HP |
| Full Shift, Analyst | ~50% | 47 HP |
| Full Shift, Veteran | 35–40% | 39 HP |
| Quick Shift | ~60% (booth-friendly) | 39 HP |
| First Shift | ~70% | 26 HP |

Other things worth holding: every player count within about 10 points of each other; roughly 6–7 comebacks per Full Shift (v28); a real share of losses before the boss (the row has to matter), but no more than about 40–45%, so the boss stays the climax; winners usually ending at a quarter to a third of their HP. `npm run balance` enforces loose bands in CI.

- **Mobs are alarms (2026-10-31).** For stronger NightBeacon CMD (SOC console) theming, every player-facing word for the enemies is now **alarm**, and a fight with two or more in play is a **multi-alarm** fight. Wording only: no rule or number changed, so no rules version or save-key bump. Code identifiers and data keys (`mobs`, `mobAdd`, `kind: 'mob'`, the `.mob` CSS) keep the old name.
- **Cards are identical in the web game and on the table (design foundation, 2026-10-30).** The user's rule: a card must read the same in the web UI as in print. The web alarm and boss cards showed a scaled hits number with a derivation note ("4 −2 · solo") and a "Routine ·" label that the printed card doesn't have. Now the web card shows the printed hits strip (1p–4p) with this crew's column marked, "Back 1–N" as printed, and pips for hits left (the coins' stand-in). A UI test compares the web strip with the printed card's text. Crew-size scaling lives only in the printed per-crew numbers and in rules, never in a web-only note.
- **No solo discount on alarm hits; solo rolls three dice (rules v29, 2026-10-30).** The user didn't want solo alarms at printed −2 hits, and wanted a solo Quick Shift to start with one agent, not two (`quickStartSolo: 1`). Solo now plays exactly the two-player numbers: `mobAdd[1]` 0 (was −2) and `bossAdd[1]` +3 (was 0), so the printed 1p and 2p columns match. To hold solo win rates, a solo analyst rolls 3 crew dice (`soloDice` 2 → 3), which is a rule, not a card change. Measured without the third die (1,200 games per mode): solo fell to Recruit 37%, Analyst 26%, Veteran 19%, Quick Shift 34%. Balance gate after (600 games per cell, 1/2/3/4 players): Recruit 63/64/64/69, Analyst 47/51/46/48, Veteran 41/40/41/41, Quick Shift 61/58/61/60, First Shift 82/71/68/69. Solo First Shift runs about 11–14 points easier than crews: the third die crushes the scripted feed's small alarms. Left as is for a teaching mode; revisit after playtests.

- **Wider comeback ranges: every alarm's range +3 (rules v28, 2026-10-30).** After a playthrough the user asked for higher persistence row odds: more comebacks, with difficulty held where it was. Every range still starts at 1; the top went up by 3. Nuisances went from 1–9/1–10 to 1–12/1–13, the heaviest from 1–4/1–5 to 1–7/1–8. Bot sims (1,200 games per cell, before the HP retune): Full Shift comebacks per game went 5.5 → 6.4 at two players, 4.8 → 5.4 at four and 6.6 → 8.4 solo; Quick Shift 2.2 → 3.0 at two players. Losses before the boss went 22% → 31% at two players. A beaten alarm still comes back only once (twice for Sticky), so wider ranges add pressure without endless loops. HP went up to hold the targets: Recruit 58 → 62, Analyst 43 → 47, Veteran 35 → 39, Quick Shift 36 → 39, First Shift 25 → 26. Balance gate (600 games per cell, 1/2/3/4 players): Recruit 61/64/64/69, Analyst 48/51/46/48, Veteran 39/40/41/41, Quick Shift 63/58/61/60, First Shift 75/71/68/69. Crew sizes are within 8 points in every mode.

- **Production audit (rules v27, 2026-10-30).** The user supplied a generic card-game audit plan (core mechanics, card graphic design, rulebook structure, design pillars, a phased roadmap). Five actions came out of it:
  - **Print text floor: 10 px (7.5 pt) for every text run on all 72 fronts and backs**, measured on rendered faces. Before, 583 runs were under 7.5 pt: stat labels, alarm flavour, a few rules lines, and the card back. A deck test now enforces the floor. To fit, the alarm art panel went 94 → 80 px on all 15 alarms, and the card-back credit wraps onto two lines. The tightest card is the Persistent Operator boss, 0.8 px inside the safe zone.
  - **Rulebook structure:** "card text beats the rulebook" up front; a What you need section moved from the end to the front; a table-layout diagram and an annotated alarm card after Setup; keywords alphabetical; an FAQ of edge cases. Core rules stay about 800 words.
  - **CMYK soft proof (`npm run proof:cmyk`).** Exports are sRGB PNG. With macOS's Generic CMYK profile, the neon accents move the most: flare #FF5A2A ΔE 21, cyan #38E1FF ΔE 18, amber #FF9B54 ΔE 13. The text accents FLT and AMT shift 10–12. Paper, ink and the dark ground hold (ΔE under 4). The light card fronts look close to the original side by side. The dark card back with neon cyan is where print will look dullest. **Don't repaint yet:** rerun with the printer's own profile (`CMYK_PROFILE=… npm run proof:cmyk`), then pick print-safe accents if the shift holds.
  - **Second wind removed.** Bot sims (1,500 games per row): at 10 HP it fired in 62–70% of games and lifted wins from behind by only 1–7 points. At 5 HP it still fired in 42–50% of games, with no from-behind gain. No threshold gave a rare comeback, so it was a flat buff, and the restraint pillar cut it. Without it every mode lost about 4 points, so HP went up: Recruit 54 → 58, Analyst 40 → 43, Veteran 32 → 35, Quick Shift 32 → 36, First Shift 23 → 25. Balance gate (600 games per cell, 1/2/3/4 players): Recruit 68/65/63/71, Analyst 53/49/49/46, Veteran 41/36/42/36, Quick Shift 65/61/61/61, First Shift 76/68/65/67. Catch-up is still open, and it has to come from player choices, not a free draw.
  - **Playtest kit (`docs/PLAYTEST.md`):** four session types, a cold-read script, an observer sheet with tags, debrief questions, and targets with the knob to turn when a table misses.

- **Pass 11: four changes from a design review (rules v25, 2026-10-30).** The user asked for a review against five goals (clear hook, emergent complexity, meaningful decisions, luck vs control, catch-up and pacing), then for all of it. Balance gate after all four (600 games per cell, 1/2/3/4 players): Recruit 64/64/63/69, Analyst 53/51/48/50, Veteran 42/35/38/37, Quick Shift 65/61/62/57, First Shift 75/67/66/64. All bands pass. The dead legacy kit code (`E.useItem`, the `forensic` bonus) went with it.
- **Guard (`guard: {min: 14}`, 2026-10-30).** "Attack or block" was never a choice: you only blocked on a double. Now, after the roll, a crew die showing 14 or more can guard instead of hitting: it blocks for whoever rolled it this round, exactly like a double's block (Piercing and arrival hits ignore it). Agent dice never guard, and a die can't guard for someone already blocking. Guarding well is worth 3–7 win-rate points to the bot (1,500 games). Engine: `E.guard(G, i)`, undone by `E.unassign` like a hit, counted in `G.stats.guards`. Print: a Guard row on the "A fight" reference card, under the doubles (the "Hits" row went: every alarm and boss prints its own 1p–4p strip).
- **Pack removed (rules v26, 2026-10-30).** Pack (v25) had the five 1-Damage alarms keep flipping to the next alarm in the feed and bring it into the fight, putting the passed situations back on top. It lifted rounds with two or more alarms from 22–29% to 29–38%. The user removed it: at a real table, reaching into the deck to pull out the next alarm is clunky. **Rule for future mechanics: nothing that makes players search, reorder or dig through the feed or kit deck; the table only ever flips the top card** (Recon's look-at-three-and-pull is the one exception, on a burn). Without Pack every mode got about 7 points easier, so HP was retuned: Recruit 60 → 54, Analyst 48 → 40, Veteran 38 → 32, Quick Shift 37 → 32 (First Shift unchanged at 23; Pack never applied there). Balance gate after (600 games per cell, 1/2/3/4 players): Recruit 66/65/65/68, Analyst 52/49/47/43, Veteran 45/36/38/35, Quick Shift 65/58/58/58, First Shift 75/67/66/64. Multi-alarm rounds are back to 22–29%; more two-alarm fights is still open, and has to come from the row, not the feed.
- **Second wind (`secondWind: {at: 10, kit: 1}`, 2026-10-30; removed in v27, above).** Crews under half HP at the halfway point won only 17–19% of Full Shifts. Now the first time anyone drops to 10 HP or less and is still standing, the crew draws one kit card, once per shift: into the tray if there's room, otherwise with the after-fight kit. Those crews now win 28%. Engine: `G.wind`, `G.stats.wind`. Print: a "Second wind" spot on the Once per fight marker, set apart under an amber "Once per shift · Don't clear" rule, so the deck stays 72 cards.
- **First Shift's scripted comeback (`intro.comeback: 2`, 2026-10-30).** The persistence row is the game's hook, and new players didn't see it bite until late. On First Shift's second flip the row isn't rolled: the MFA Banshee comes back (row result 1), hits everyone for 1 on the way in, and is fought after Patch Tuesday. First Shift HP drops 29 → 23 and 4-player alarm hits go from printed + 4 to printed + 3 (`mobAdd`). Print: the First Shift card marks flip 2 "+ Banshee back" and says what happens; `intro.tips['2']` tells it in the web game.
- **Solo draws one kit card per won fight (`kitSolo: 1`, 2026-10-30).** The user found solo kit draws too generous: a solo Engineer drew about 8.6 kit cards per Quick Shift, more than half the 16-card deck (bot, 2,000 games). Crews still draw two; a solo Soarcerer draws two. One kit card alone cut solo win rates by 15–20 points, so solo scaling moved to `mobAdd[1]` −2 and `bossAdd[1]` 0 (were −1 and +1). Solo after the change, 1,500 games per mode (2026-10-30): Recruit 66%, Analyst 54%, Veteran 40%, Quick Shift 59%, First Shift 67%, in line with the other crew sizes. HP is unchanged.
- **The kit deck reshuffles (`reshuffleKit`, 2026-10-29).** Before this, the kit deck ran dry in 32% of Full Shifts, so the late fights, the ones that matter, paid nothing. Now, when a draw needs more cards than the kit deck holds, the discard pile (burned and dropped agents) is shuffled into a new kit deck. The engine logs "The kit deck is empty: the burned agents are shuffled into a new one." and counts it in `G.stats.reshuffles`. The rule sits on the "A fight" reference card's Won line and in the Kit section of the core rules.
- **Core rules and advanced modules (`advanced`, `G.adv`, 2026-10-29).** The rules had grown to about 2,300 words: too much to teach at a booth, where a stranger has a minute of attention before the first flip. First Shift and Quick Shift now play the **core** rules, about one printed page: no story cards and no boss row triggers. Each boss keeps the rest of its ability (Lockjaw's +2 damage, the Operator's comeback, the Rogue AI's agent shutdown). Full Shift adds the **advanced modules**: story cards (3 per feed) and boss row triggers. `docs/RULES.md` is split the same way (core, advanced, reference). Print: each boss card leads its row-trigger sentence with an amber "Full Shift:", story cards carry "Advanced · Full Shift" on their tag line, and the row mat's setup line names the split.
- **Components for a physical release (2026-10-29).** 6 crew d20s in one colour, 3 agent d20s in a second colour, 1 row d20 in amber, and coins (2 per HP tracker plus about 20 for hits). A four-player round with a Triage burn can need 9–10 dice plus the row die; agent dice get their own colour because they can't hit the boss and the Engineer gives them +3.
- **A marker sheet: 72 cards on eight sheets (2026-10-29).** The web build tracks state a table has to remember by hand, so an eighth sheet of 9 marker cards (`meta` type `marker`) carries it: 6 **Came back** cards (amber, tucked under a comeback so only a "BACK" band shows above it; it decides the Hunter's +3, Hunt Agent targets and gone-for-good), 1 **Next alarm** card (Defense and Damage coin tracks for a story card's mark), 1 **Round** card (a 1–8 track for Escalating and Lockjaw's trigger) and 1 **Once per fight** card (Decoy used, Operator risen). Each has the shared card back. The PDF is 16 Letter pages; the singles export prints the Came back front × 6.
- **Analyst retune (2026-10-29).** Two-player crews led by up to 16 points in Full Shift, and the win-rate spread across analyst pairs was 43–69%. After it, two-player crews sit with the other crew sizes, but pairs still run 37–60% (1,500 games per pair, Analyst HP, 2026-10-29): Engineer pairs sit lowest and Hunter + Soarcerer highest, so pair synergy is the next thing to tune. `perks` now: Hunter +3 against comebacks (was +5), and comebacks it or a Hunt Agent finishes are gone for good; Responder +3 against the highest-damage alarm, boss included (was +2), Containment cuts 1 more; Engineer doubles on 17–20, agent dice +3; Soarcerer +1 tray slot and +1 kit card per won fight; Fixer heals 5 split after every fight (was 4) and 3 split off-shift, Recovery heals 3 more. The class `perk` text and the cards' "Your move" lines print the numbers from `perks`.
- **`bossAdd` 2p goes to +3: {1: +1, 2: +3, 3: +4, 4: +6}** (was +2 at two players), with `mobAdd` unchanged at {1: −1, 2: 0, 3: +2, 4: +4}. With the analyst retune it brings two-player crews back into line. - **Analyst redesign: a perk and an agent link each (2026-10-29).** The user asked for more interesting, more balanced analysts. Solo, they ran from 40% to 73% (Full Shift, Analyst): the Responder's +5 on the highest-damage alarm landed on the boss in every fight, so a solo Responder always had it, and the Fixer's off-shift heal did nothing for a crew of one. Now every analyst has a perk and an agent link, both text on the class (`perk`, `agent`) with the numbers in `perks` in `data/cards.json`:

| --- | --- | --- |

- **Agent links: every analyst makes one kind of automation better.** The design intent is that automation is how a SOC survives the night, and the analysts should teach that too. Each agent link boosts one agent type (Hunt, Containment, the rolling agents, the tray and draw, Recovery), and it works for the whole crew's agents, not just the analyst's own. So a crew of analysts builds agent synergies: a Hunter wants Hunt Agents, an Engineer wants Triage and Hunt, a Fixer wants Recovery, and the Soarcerer feeds all of them.
- **Boss hits by crew size: `bossAdd` {1: +1, 2: +2, 3: +4, 4: +6}** (2p since raised to +3, above) on the printed `hitsBase`, replacing "printed + 1 per analyst". The boss fight is a big share of a short game, so in Quick Shift and First Shift a flat +1 per analyst let crew sizes drift apart; a per-count table, like `mobAdd` for alarms, keeps the short modes even across crew sizes. Boss cards print all four numbers in a hit strip like the alarm cards, and have no hit boxes.

- **Why players kept seeing Lockjaw (a bug, not the pick).** The start screen defaulted to First Shift, which always uses the Lockjaw Syndicate (`intro.boss`), and "Run it back" replays the same mode. The engine's pick is fair in Full and Quick Shift: about a third each over 3,000 games.
- **Story cards make the Recon Agent matter (2026-10-29).** The user asked for more story-driven cards that make Recon more impactful. Nine situations are now story cards (`story: true`), each with a `recon` effect and a `reconText` line. If the crew holds a running Recon Agent when one resolves, the Recon line replaces the whole outcome: no roll, no choice (`G.lastSit.recon`, counted in `G.stats.auto.recon`). Every Full Shift feed deals 3 story cards and Quick Shift 2 (`storyDeal`); First Shift has none, so the tutorial stays fixed. Story cards bring four new effects: `row` (roll for the row now; comebacks strike on arrival and are fought after the card), `call` (the widest-range alarm in the row comes back with no arrival hit), `nextMob` (the next freshly flipped alarm gets a Defense or Damage change until beaten) and `bury` (the next situation goes to the bottom of the feed). The Recon Agent's run now also gives a story card's Recon line; its burn looks at the next 3 situations and pulls one from the feed for good instead of burying it. In bot sims a Recon-favouring bot no longer loses win rate against the default bot, and its burn picks out the heavy hitters. Print: story cards carry an amber "Story" tag and frame, a shorter art panel, and a separate cyan "With Recon" box with an eye icon, so the table can see at a glance which line applies.
- **Bosses roll for the row (2026-10-29).** The user asked for boss abilities that trigger the persistence row more often. Lockjaw rolls for the row at the end of every round it's still up; the Persistent Operator rolls on every double that hits it; the Rogue AI rolls whenever an agent is burned. These are normal Row rolls (arrival strikes capped at two, plog `why` is `'boss'`), unlike the boss's own arrival roll, which brings comebacks in without the hit. With story cards and boss triggers together, losses before the boss fell to 30–43%, down from 63–70%: the boss is the climax again, not a formality after the row has already decided the shift.

The count is an invariant: `deck/build.js`, the singles export and the deck tests all check it, and changing it changes the sheet layout.
- **A steeper alarm-hits table: `mobAdd` {1: −1, 2: 0, 3: +2, 4: +4}** (solo since −2, above) on the printed 2-player hits, never below 1. It replaces {−1, 0, +1, +2}; with boss row triggers and story cards, the steeper table for big crews keeps every player count inside its band.
- **One HP number per difficulty.** Crew size is absorbed by the dice rule (one die each, two for on point) and alarm hits (the `mobAdd` table). Don't reintroduce per-count HP tables; fix spread with scaling instead.
- **Comebacks return at full hits, and still strike on arrival.** The user's call (2026-10-29): an alarm that persists should come back as strong as it left, so persistence feels like real persistence. The old one-hit comeback made the row a speed bump. The arrival hit stays capped at the two highest-Damage comebacks per roll (`strikeCap`); the `returnHits` key is gone.
- **Automation is how a SOC survives the night.** The game is balanced so the AI agents carry real weight, and the design intent is that it teaches exactly that. Bot sims on 2026-10-29 (1,200 games per player count, the balance-gate setup): without agents the bot wins 1–2% of games; with them, about 50%. In an average two-player Analyst game the agents land about 10 hits, set up 12 more crew hits (Enrichment and Recon bonuses), stop 19 damage (Containment and Decoy), heal 22 HP (Recovery), and remove or dodge about 2.5 comebacks (Hunt and Patch). The engine counts these in `G.stats.auto` so the web game can show them. To get there, a won fight draws two kit cards (`kitPerFight`), the crew tray holds five (six with the Soarcerer), and the agents got stronger: Triage +4, Enrichment +3, Containment cuts 2, Recovery heals 4, and the Hunt Agent now hunts comebacks (+7, only against an alarm that came back from the row; burn beats one).
- **Tougher bosses: +2 hits and +2 damage each.** Lockjaw 7 hits / 9 damage, Persistent Operator 7 / 9, Rogue AI 6 / 9, then printed + 1 per analyst (now the `bossAdd` table, above). Stronger agents would otherwise flatten the finale; with this, about 63–70% of losses still came before the boss (since brought down to 30–43% by boss row triggers, above).
- **HP retuned for full comebacks and stronger agents** (superseded by the story-card retune above): Recruit 70, Analyst 60, Veteran 47, Quick Shift 37, First Shift 25. The HP tracker became a tens track plus a ones track with two coins per analyst, since one cell per HP point no longer fits a card.
- **Only the two highest-Damage comebacks of a roll strike.** Every range starts at 1, so a roll of 1–4 empties the row. Uncapped, 6.7% of Row rolls hit one analyst for 10+ before any choice (1,500 two-player Analyst games, 2026-10-29); capped at two, 0.6%. Comebacks per shift stayed at about 8, and Full Shift HP dropped from 50/42/34 to 44/38/31 to hold the targets. `strikeCap` in `data/cards.json`.
- **The row rolls with the flip,** so comebacks join the fight in front of you rather than becoming a separate fight.
- **Tougher alarms: Defense floor of 12, more hits.** Alarms at 8 felt free, and at 11 they still fell too fast. In 2,000 two-player Analyst games on 2026-10-29, heavy hitters fell in their first round 8–12% of the time, down from 23–31%, and nuisances 40–49%, down from about 70%. Full Shift HP rose to 59/50/41 (Quick Shift 31, First Shift 20) to hold the targets. Quick Shift fights grew from about 10.5 to 12.9 combat rounds, so the booth length has to be re-timed with real players.
- **Alarm hits come from a per-count table, not +1 per analyst.** `mobAdd` in `data/cards.json` is now {1: −1, 2: 0, 3: +2, 4: +4} on the printed 2-player hits, never below 1 (see the steeper-table entry above; it was {−1, 0, +1, +2} with full-hit comebacks and the bigger crew tray). Each alarm card prints all four numbers, and has no hit boxes: one row of boxes can only be right for one crew size. Boss scaling now uses its own table, `bossAdd` (above).
- **The shift runs 22:00 to 06:00: survive until dawn.** Flip i of N shows 22:00 + (i − 1) × 480 / N minutes, rounded down to 5; the boss is always 05:59. Times are positional because the feed is shuffled, so they live on the row mat and in the web game, never on a card.
- **Read-aloud cards.** Whoever's on point reads the flipped card aloud. Flavour lines are short second-person scenes (1–2 sentences, 140 characters at most), so the table hears the night instead of reading it.
- **Containment can't reduce damage below 1; Decoy protects only whoever's on point.** Both were erasing whole rounds.
- **Kit is all dual-mode agents (run or burn) in one shared tray.** Per-player slots let bigger crews snowball.
- **Each alarm has its own comeback range.** Nuisances come back often (1–10), heavy hitters rarely (1–4 to 1–6). Every range starts at 1, so the Patch Agent's keep-the-highest die still helps against all of them. The Threat Intel Drop removes the widest one.
- **Beacon comebacks chain.** Anything a Beacon's roll brings back rolls the row again, until a roll comes up quiet. About one chain roll per Full Shift; it's the swing that makes Beacons feel like C2.
- **Keywords over bespoke abilities.** Eight words to learn once. The PsExec Swarm's spread folded into Beacon, the Rootkit's wide range into Regen, the Vibe Coded App's hallucinations into Escalating.
